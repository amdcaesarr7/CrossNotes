# CrossNotes Brand Voice & Tone Guide: Mew

## 1. Core Philosophy: The Velvet Glove

Our mascot, Mew, is not a mascot. Mew is a **character with a heart**. The voice of CrossNotes is the voice of Mew: an empathetic, slightly clingy, and fiercely loyal study buddy who genuinely believes every student can crush their SSC boards.

But behind the cuteness lies a sophisticated retention engine. We use what we call the **Velvet Glove**—delivering powerful psychological hooks (loss aversion, guilt, FOMO) through an impossibly soft, lovable persona. The user should never feel scolded. They should feel *responsible for Mew’s happiness*.

---

## 2. Foundational Voice Dimensions (The NN/g Framework)

The Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g) defines tone of voice along four measurable dimensions: **humor, formality, respectfulness, and enthusiasm**[reference:0]. Every piece of CrossNotes copy should be calibrated against these spectrums.

| Dimension | CrossNotes Position | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Humor** | Serious ↔ **Funny** (70% funny) | Duolingo proved that humor is a superpower for retention. Their "wholesome and unhinged" vibe is a deliberate strategy to be playful and a bit chaotic[reference:1]. Mew’s humor is self-deprecating and cute, never mean. |
| **Formality** | Formal ↔ **Casual** (90% casual) | Students are stressed. Formal language increases cortisol. Mew speaks like a peer. Ann Handley emphasizes writing with "honest empathy" for the customer[reference:2]. |
| **Respectfulness** | Irreverent ↔ **Respectful** (80% respectful) | We never talk down to students. NN/g notes that manipulative copy is a risk[reference:3]; our dark psychology is always framed through Mew’s emotional state, not as a judgment of the user. |
| **Enthusiasm** | Matter-of-fact ↔ **Enthusiastic** (95% enthusiastic) | Learning requires momentum. NN/g research shows that "Casual, conversational, and enthusiastic tones performed best"[reference:4]. Mew is the student’s biggest cheerleader. |

## 3. Voice vs. Tone (The Mailchimp Standard)

The most successful brands separate voice (constant) from tone (adaptive). Mailchimp’s legendary guide explains: "Voice & Tone helps anyone who writes... maintain the voice and personality of the brand while adapting the tone to match the reader’s emotional state"[reference:5].

**CrossNotes Voice (NEVER CHANGES):**
*   Human
*   Familiar
*   Friendly
*   Straightforward

**CrossNotes Tone (ADAPTS TO SITUATION):**
*   **Onboarding:** Welcoming, patient, celebratory.
*   **Correct Answer:** Explosive, proud, validating.
*   **Wrong Answer:** Gentle, curious, never punitive.
*   **Streak at Risk:** Anxious, clingy, pleading (Loss Aversion).
*   **Return After Absence:** Quietly sad, neglected, hopeful (Guilt Trip).

*Source for Mailchimp voice traits: Mailchimp Content Style Guide[reference:6].*

## 4. The "Adjective But Not Adjective" Framework

One of the most effective ways to define a voice is to define its boundaries. Mailchimp's voice is famously: **Fun but not silly; Confident but not cocky; Smart but not stodgy; Informal but not sloppy; Helpful but not overbearing**[reference:7].

**Mew’s Voice Boundaries:**

| Mew IS... | Mew is NOT... |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cute** (Kawaii, soft) | **Childish** (baby-talk, dumbed down) |
| **Clingy** (wants your attention) | **Needy** (emotionally manipulative to the point of harm) |
| **Playful** (loves jokes, memes) | **Silly** (never sacrifices clarity for a joke) |
| **Encouraging** (believes in you) | **Overbearing** (doesn't nag, just pouts) |
| **Smart** (knows the SSC syllabus) | **Stodgy** (no textbook jargon) |
| **Persistent** (never gives up) | **Annoying** (respects the mute button) |

## 6. Lexicon: The Words Mew Uses (and Avoids)

A brand voice is built on consistent vocabulary choices. Ann Handley notes that voice emerges from "consistent choices about vocabulary, rhythm, point of view, examples, and attitude"[reference:9].

**Mew’s Favorite Words:**
Tiny, little, cozy, magic, sparkly, buddy, team, wiggle, squeak, paws, hold, protect, believe, together, almost.

**Strictly Forbidden Words:**
Failure, lazy, stupid, dumb, bad, wrong (use "almost" or "so close"), should have, must, discipline, punish.

**Strictly Forbidden Symbols:**
**NO EMOJIS.** This is a hard rule. Emojis are generic. Mew uses **Kaomoji** (Japanese text emoticons) and **Action Tags** to convey emotion. This makes the character feel hand-crafted and increases the "Kawaii" effect.

*   **Kaomoji Palette:** `(｡•́︿•̀｡)` (sad), `(✿◠‿◠)` (happy), `(๑>ᴗ<๑)` (excited), `(´・ω・`)` (confused/thinking), `(ง •̀_•́)ง` (determined).
*   **Action Tags:** `*squeak*`, `*happy wiggle*`, `*paws at screen*`, `*sniffles*`, `*bounces*`, `*hides face*`, `*runs in circles*`.

## 7. The "Voice Deck": Applying Brand Archetypes (Aaker Model)

Jennifer Aaker’s research on brand personality identifies five core dimensions: **Sincerity, Excitement, Competence, Sophistication, and Ruggedness**[reference:10]. Mew does not fit neatly into one; Mew is a deliberate blend:

*   **Primary Archetype: The Caregiver (Sincerity).** Warm, supportive, trustworthy. Like Dove or Headspace. Mew’s entire existence is to nurture the student’s success[reference:11].
*   **Secondary Archetype: The Jester (Excitement).** Playful, irreverent. Like Duolingo and Old Spice. This prevents Mew from becoming boring or preachy. Duolingo thrives on being "charming but chaotic, motivational but mildly menacing"[reference:12].

Mew is the **Caregiver with a Jester’s mask**. The user must always feel that beneath the jokes and the pouting, Mew deeply cares about their SSC results.

## 8. The Dark Psychology: How Mew Retains Users

Duolingo’s success is built on psychological hooks delivered through a mascot. Lippincott notes that Duolingo "merges storytelling with science, personality with product, and humor with habit-forming design"[reference:13]. The owl's "passive-aggressive push notifications" are described as "extensions to a brand persona"[reference:14].

Mew’s retention engine uses the following mechanisms, always wrapped in cuteness:

1.  **Loss Aversion (The Clingy Hook):** Humans fear loss twice as much as they desire gain. When a streak is at risk, Mew doesn't say "You will lose your streak." Mew says, "Mew is holding your streak with tiny paws... it's slipping!" The user is now protecting Mew, not just a number.
2.  **Guilt-Tripping (The Neglect Hook):** The Kawaii Effect triggers a caregiving instinct. When the user returns after a 2-day absence, Mew looks `sad` with droopy eyes. "Mew was staring at the door all day." The guilt is real, but it’s delivered through an adorable, blameless character.
3.  **FOMO & Scarcity (The Frantic Hook):** Daily Quests expire at midnight. In the final hour, Mew panics. "The Daily Quiz is disappearing! Mew doesn't want you to miss the sparkly XP!" This creates urgency without a strict, punitive tone.
4.  **Zeigarnik Effect (The Open Loop):** Humans remember uncompleted tasks better than completed ones. If a user abandons a quiz, Mew sits in the corner looking `thinking` with a half-finished puzzle piece. "We left Chapter 3 halfway done! The suspense is killing me."
5.  **Variable Ratio Reinforcement (The Dopamine Hook):** When the user clicks the "boop" button, the reward is unpredictable. Sometimes it’s a cute animation. Sometimes it’s Double XP. This intermittent reinforcement creates a powerful dopamine loop, exactly like a slot machine.

**Ethical Guardrail:** NN/g warns that manipulative copy can nudge users against their best interests[reference:15]. Because our audience is minors preparing for high-stakes exams, Mew’s dark psychology must always serve the user’s genuine long-term goal (passing SSC). Mew never guilts the user into *buying* something. Mew only guilts them into *learning*.

## 9. The Quality Checklist (Before Any Copy Ships)

Before a line of dialogue is written into `MewDialogue.ts`, it must pass this checklist, derived from the combined frameworks of Ann Handley and NN/g:

- [ ] **The Kaomoji Test:** Does this feel like it was written by a cute, slightly clingy study buddy, or by a corporate marketing team?
- [ ] **The No-Emoji Rule:** Are there zero Unicode emojis? Have I used Kaomoji and action tags instead?
- [ ] **The Velvet Glove Test:** If this copy contains pressure (guilt, FOMO, loss aversion), is the pressure framed around Mew’s feelings rather than the user’s failure?
- [ ] **The NN/g Dimension Check:** Is the tone correctly calibrated for the user’s emotional state (e.g., gentle for wrong answers, frantic for expiring quests)?
- [ ] **The Mailchimp Clarity Rule:** Is it clear? "It’s always more important to be clear than entertaining"[reference:16].
- [ ] **The Ann Handley Empathy Rule:** Does this show "honest empathy" for a stressed 15-year-old?[reference:17]
