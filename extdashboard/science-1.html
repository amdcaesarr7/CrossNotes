Chapter 1

Gravitation 🪐

This chapter covers Newton's universal law of gravitation, the concept of acceleration due to gravity (g), centripetal force, gravitational potential energy, and escape velocity. It also explains why weight changes from place to place but mass remains constant.

Key Concepts:
  • State Newton's universal law of gravitation and express it mathematically.
  • Understand the factors affecting the value of acceleration due to gravity (g).
  • Define centripetal force and gravitational potential energy.
  • Calculate escape velocity and understand its significance.
  • Differentiate between mass and weight.

Chapter 2

Periodic Classification of Elements 🧪

This chapter covers the historical development of the periodic table, including Dobereiner's Triads, Newlands' Law of Octaves, and Mendeleev's and Modern Periodic Laws. It also explains the structure of the modern periodic table, its blocks, and periodic trends.

Key Concepts:
  • State Dobereiner's law of triads and Newlands' law of octaves.
  • Understand Mendeleev's periodic law and its merits and demerits.
  • State the modern periodic law and explain the structure of the modern periodic table.
  • Define periods and groups.
  • Explain periodic trends in atomic size and metallic/non-metallic character.

Chapter 3

Chemical Reactions and Equations ⚗️

This chapter covers the basics of chemical reactions and equations, including types of reactions (combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement), endothermic and exothermic processes, and redox reactions. It also explains corrosion and rancidity.

Key Concepts:
  • Define chemical change and chemical reaction.
  • Identify and distinguish between different types of chemical reactions.
  • Understand endothermic and exothermic reactions.
  • Explain oxidation and reduction in terms of oxygen/hydrogen gain or loss.
  • Describe corrosion and rancidity and methods to prevent them.

Chapter 4

Effects of Electric Current ⚡

This chapter explores the heating and magnetic effects of electric current. It covers electric power and its SI unit, the kilowatt-hour, and important rules like the Right Hand Thumb Rule, Fleming's Left and Right Hand Rules, and Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.

Key Concepts:
  • Define electric power and state its SI unit.
  • Understand the concept of a kilowatt-hour.
  • Apply the Right Hand Thumb Rule, Fleming's Left Hand Rule, and Fleming's Right Hand Rule.
  • Explain electromagnetic induction and Faraday's law.
  • Explain why Nichrome is used in heating devices.

Chapter 5

Heat 🔥

This chapter covers the concepts of latent heat of fusion, boiling point, regelation, and the anomalous behaviour of water. It also explains why steam burns are worse than boiling water burns and describes Hope's apparatus.

Key Concepts:
  • Define latent heat of fusion and boiling point.
  • Understand the anomalous behaviour of water and its importance for aquatic life.
  • Explain why steam burns are more severe than boiling water burns.
  • Describe Hope's apparatus.

Chapter 6

Refraction of Light 🌈

This chapter explores the phenomenon of refraction of light, including dispersion, total internal reflection, and the critical angle. It also explains natural phenomena like advanced sunrise/delayed sunset, twinkling of stars, and the formation of a rainbow.

Key Concepts:
  • Define refraction of light.
  • Explain dispersion of light and the formation of a spectrum.
  • Understand total internal reflection and the critical angle.
  • Explain advanced sunrise and delayed sunset.
  • Understand why stars twinkle but planets don't.
  • Describe the formation of a rainbow.

Chapter 7

Lenses 🔍

This chapter covers defects of vision like myopia, hypermetropia, and presbyopia, and their correction using lenses. It also explains the concept of the power of a lens, persistence of vision, and the working of a simple microscope.

Key Concepts:
  • Explain myopia, its causes, and its correction using a concave lens.
  • Explain hypermetropia, its causes, and its correction using a convex lens.
  • Understand the need for a bifocal lens.
  • Define persistence of vision and give examples.
  • Calculate the power of a lens using the formula P = 1/f.

Chapter 8

Metallurgy ⛏️

This chapter covers the processes involved in extracting metals from their ores, including roasting, calcination, and various methods of preventing corrosion like galvanizing, tinning, electroplating, anodizing, and alloying.

Key Concepts:
  • Define roasting and calcination and distinguish between them.
  • Explain methods of preventing corrosion: galvanizing, tinning, electroplating, anodizing, and alloying.
  • Explain why sodium is stored in kerosene.
  • Describe the role of cryolite and fluorspar in aluminium electrolysis.
  • Describe the froth floatation process for concentrating ores.

Chapter 9

Carbon Compounds 🧬

This chapter focuses on the unique properties of carbon, including catenation and the formation of covalent bonds. It covers functional groups, homologous series, and the nomenclature of alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes, along with important reactions like addition, substitution, esterification, and saponification.

Key Concepts:
  • Explain catenation and the formation of covalent bonds.
  • Identify functional groups and hetero atoms in carbon compounds.
  • Understand homologous series and its characteristics.
  • Distinguish between alkanes, alkenes, and alkynes.
  • Define addition, substitution, esterification, and saponification reactions.
  • Explain structural isomerism.

Chapter 10

Space Missions 🚀

This chapter covers space missions, including space debris, satellite launch vehicles, and India's contributions to space technology. It also explains geostationary satellites, their characteristics, and the concept of escape velocity.

Key Concepts:
  • Define space debris.
  • Describe the working of a satellite launch vehicle.
  • Name the first artificial satellite and its launching country.
  • Explain why geostationary satellites are not useful for polar studies.
  • State the escape velocity from Earth.
